Welcome to the Super Telmi build guide. You are about to give your children the very best storytelling box there is not only because of the love you will put into making them this beautiful gift, but also because of the outstanding durability of the hardware offered here, as well as the unlimited potential offered by Telmi.

# Required equipment

* Electronic soldering equipment
* 3D printer
* [A Miyoo Mini Plus](https://wiki.telmi.fr/achats/miyoo_mini_plus)

## 3D printing the parts

![](assets/super_telmi_case_3d.gif)

The 3D files are available [HERE](../files/) and must be printed in TPU for shock absorption.

For the main shell, an infill of at least 25% to 30% is recommended to make sure the Super Telmi is as resistant as possible to knocks, drops, temper tantrums, or any other creative misuse aimed at doing violence to a sibling.

A higher infill would risk making the shell too rigid, and therefore less effective at absorbing impacts.

## Disassembling the Miyoo

The first step is to remove the battery, through the small hatch at the back of the Miyoo.
It comes out fairly easily using small pliers, gently pull out the 2-pin connector.

Let's start taking off the Miyoo's back shell. You will spot 6 small M2 screws.

![](assets/miyoo_back_01.png)

Once they are removed, take off the shell (by prying it gently), you will not need it anymore.

The screen is held by a ribbon cable, which you need to disconnect. To do so, locate the small white line on the motherboard and unlock it by prying it open. Then gently pull the ribbon cable out to disconnect the screen.

![](assets/miyoo_mother_board_ribbon_screen_cable_01.png)

![](assets/miyoo_mother_board_ribbon_screen_cable_02.png)

![](assets/miyoo_mother_board_ribbon_screen_cable_03.png)

![](assets/miyoo_back_02.png)

Now take out all the small parts of the Miyoo so you can look them over and get familiar with them:

![](assets/miyoo_buttons_01.png)

* The buttons
* The D-pads and their silicone supports
* The triggers (which will no longer be used, as they are unnecessary for Telmi)
* And finally the power and volume control buttons (which are barely held in place, as you will see)

Set all of this carefully aside.

## Preparing the speaker

It is now time to remove the speaker, located at the bottom right. This little component offers very poor sound quality. It makes a simple pressure contact on the motherboard through its two legs, and that is exactly what we are going to reproduce with our 40 mm speaker.

![](assets/miyoo_hp_01.png)

Now that all the components are accessible, you can set aside the back shell, the triggers and the original speaker: they are of no use.

![](assets/miyoo_spares_01.png)

Using wires roughly 20 cm long, solder them to the speaker.

![](assets/miyoo_hp_02.png)

Then feed the wires through the speaker hole in the Super Telmi case. Tip: you can fit a piece of foam here to seat the speaker, which will improve both the acoustics and the stability.

![](assets/miyoo_hp_03.png)

Now gently turn the motherboard over to start soldering onto the speaker connectors. They are at the bottom right, marked "SPK1".

Feel free to run an audio test by powering on the motherboard while pressing against the contact pads before soldering. Don't worry, the voltage of the device is very low, you are in no danger.

> The polarity of the speaker connections does not matter here.

![](assets/miyoo_hp_04.png)

## Assembling the Super Telmi

The speaker is ready, so you can now carefully position the motherboard inside the case. Slide the speaker wires along the edge of the Miyoo.
You can use a bit of adhesive tape to hold the wiring in place all the way to the speaker.

![](assets/miyoo_hp_05.png)

Once you are happy with the speaker installation and the placement of the motherboard, calmly reassemble all the small components. Be gentle, this takes patience. The buttons are particularly unstable, so stay attentive.

A good tip is to use masking tape to hold all the components in place before starting the assembly.

![](assets/miyoo_buttons_02.png)

Everything in place? Position the back panel, hold it, then gently turn the machine over.
You can reuse the original screws to tighten it all up. Tighten in a crosswise pattern.

Finally, fit the speaker front panel.

![](assets/miyoo_hp_06.png)

Test everything! You should now have a Super Telmi ready to use.

![](assets/super_telmi_01.png)

Have fun customising the space on the back if you like, for example the child's first name, your contact details in case the device gets lost, etc. You can edit the text directly in your favourite slicer thanks to the 3MF file.

![](assets/super_telmi_label.png)

## Building the docking station

Once your station has been printed and cleaned up, you can apply one drop (no more) of cyanoacrylate glue in each hole made for the neodymium magnets.

> Double-check the polarity, orientation matters, before sealing them permanently.

![](assets/nopreview.png)

Now, insert an M3 bolt from the back of the station into the hole provided for it.

![](assets/nopreview.png)

With the bolt in place, fit a small cable tie around it, which you can partially close, leaving it just open enough to pass the head of the USB-C cable and its magnetic tip through.

![](assets/nopreview.png)

Once you have carefully worked out the ideal position with the Super Telmi, tighten the cable tie firmly to hold the cable and its magnetic tip permanently in place.

![](assets/nopreview.png)

Cut off the excess cable tie with wire cutters, then tuck the USB cable along the slot provided for it.

![](assets/nopreview.png)

Congratulations, your Super Telmi is finished. All that's left is to wrap it up as a gift 🎁
